SECTION 1: PROJECT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

PROJECT NAME: FAIRDEAL — AI-Powered Employment Contract Intelligence Engine
TAGLINE: "Drop a contract. Get the truth."

WHAT IT DOES:
FairDeal takes an employment contract (PDF or DOCX), runs it through an
8-stage automated analysis pipeline, and produces:
  - Extracted key terms (salary, notice period, bonds, non-compete, probation)
  - Market salary percentile benchmarking against 2,795 real salary records
  - Red flag detection with severity levels and actionable recommendations
  - Favorable term identification
  - A psychological fairness score (0-100) with letter grade
  - Context-aware negotiation playbook with ready-to-use email scripts
  - RAG-powered clause evidence from a knowledge base of 30 real contracts
  - AI-generated or deterministic narration verdict
All in under 5 seconds.

THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES:
Every year millions of Indian graduates sign contracts they don't understand.
Buried in legalese are training bonds worth Rs.2,00,000+, 90-day notice
periods, aggressive non-competes, and missing statutory benefits — all
designed to lock employees in. No tool in the market combines contract
parsing + market benchmarking + legal risk analysis + negotiation scripting.

SECTION 4: THE 8-STAGE PIPELINE (DEEP TECHNICAL DETAIL)
================================================================================

STAGE 1 — PARSE + OCR (parser_service.py, ocr_service.py)
  - Accepts PDF or DOCX via multipart upload (max 10MB)
  - PDF: Uses pdfplumber to extract text page-by-page
  - DOCX: Uses python-docx to extract paragraphs
  - Computes "text density" per page (non-whitespace / total chars)
  - If density < 0.1 OR text < 200 chars → document is scanned
  - Scanned PDFs: Converts pages to PNG via PyMuPDF (fitz) at 150 DPI,
    sends each as base64 to Gemini 2.0 Flash Vision API for OCR
  - Output: ParsedDocument with full_text, pages[], density scores

STAGE 2 — HYBRID EXTRACTION (rule_extraction_service.py, sniper_extraction_service.py)
  FIRST PASS — Deterministic Regex (697 lines of battle-tested patterns):
    - CTC: 6 priority tiers of regex patterns
      1. LPA patterns (highest priority — most common in Indian contracts)
      2. Explicit CTC/annual mentions
      3. Monthly salary → annual conversion
      4. Large INR amounts with currency symbols
      5. Fixed + Variable breakdowns
      Context exclusion: Ignores gratuity/insurance/coverage amounts
    - Notice Period: 7 tiers of patterns
      1. Explicit "notice period is X months"
      2. "giving X months notice"
      3. Generic "X months notice"
      4. "notice of X days"
      5. Termination/resignation section scan
      6. "salary in lieu of notice"
      7. Last resort proximity search (notice near X months)
      Handles: hyphenated forms, word numbers, all quote variants
    - Bond: 4 phases
      1. Bond keyword BEFORE amount
      2. Amount THEN bond keyword within 150 chars
      3. Service agreement with amount
      4. Bond as months of salary (negative sentinel → post-processed)
      Cross-validation: bond must NOT equal salary (catches false positives)
    - Non-compete: Patterns for non-compete, non-solicitation, restrictive covenant
    - Probation: Numeric and word-based extraction
    - Benefits: 12 categories regex-matched (health, PF, gratuity, leave, etc.)
    - Role & Company: Pattern + known company list (27 major Indian companies)

  SECOND PASS — Sniper LLM (if ANY critical field missing):
    - Page scoring algorithm: rewards pages containing relevant keywords,
      penalizes pages with irrelevant terms (gratuity, insurance)
    - Sends ONLY top 2 most relevant pages to Gemini (not the full doc)
    - extract_all(): Single comprehensive LLM call with strict JSON schema
    - Merge strategy: LLM fills gaps only, regex results take priority
    - Anti-hallucination: temperature=0.0, topP=0.0, topK=1,
      response_mime_type="application/json"

  DATA SANITIZATION PIPELINE:
    - String-to-number conversion (handles Indian format: 18,00,000)
    - LPA-to-INR conversion (values < 200 → multiply by 100,000)
    - Monthly-to-annual correction (values < 10,000 → multiply by 12)
    - Upper-bound sanity check (> 50 Crore → discard as garbage)
    - NaN/Infinity protection

STAGE 3 — MARKET BENCHMARKING (benchmark_service.py)
  - Loads 2,795 records from 23 JSON files into a pandas DataFrame
  - Role normalization: 60+ role aliases mapped to 6 categories
    (sde, service_engineer, analyst, operations, marketing, hr, finance)
  - Progressive Cohort Broadening (4-step):
    Step 0: Filter by role category (needs >= 5 matches)
    Step 1: Filter by company type + location (relax if < 5)
    Step 2: Filter by experience ±1 year (relax if < 5)
    Step 3: Filter by industry (relax if < 5)
    Fallback: Use full national dataset
  - Computes: exact percentile, P25, P50, P75, mean, cohort size
  - Notice period percentile: separate cohort with ±2 year window
  - Interpretation: excellent(>80), above_avg(>60), avg(>40), below_avg(>20), poor

STAGE 4 — RED FLAG & FAVORABLE TERM DETECTION (red_flag_service.py)
  Categories checked:
  | Category     | Red Flags                                          | Favorable      |
  |------------- |--------------------------------------------------- |--------------- |
  | Salary       | <10th pct (critical), <25th pct (high)             | >75th pct      |
  | Notice       | >80th pct (high), >60th pct (medium)               | <20th pct      |
  | Non-Compete  | >12mo (critical), 7-12mo (high), 1-6mo (medium)   | —              |
  | Bond         | >=2L (critical), 50K-2L (high), <50K (low)         | —              |
  | Probation    | >6 months (medium)                                 | <=3 months     |
  | Benefits     | <=1 benefit (medium)                               | >=4, >=6       |

  Each flag includes: severity, impact_score, market_context, recommendation

STAGE 5 — PSYCHOLOGICAL SCORING ENGINE v3.0 (psychological_scoring.py)
  5 Component Scores (each 0-100):
    1. Salary Score: exponential reward curve (percentile → 20-100 points)
    2. Notice Score: inverted sigmoid (lower percentile = shorter = better)
    3. Benefits Score: tiered (0→30, 4→80, 8→100) + premium bonus + mandatory penalty
    4. Clauses Score: starts at 100, deductions for non-compete/bond/garden leave
    5. Legal Compliance: starts at 100, deductions for PF/gratuity absence, >48hr/week

  Dynamic Weights (shift by context):
    Entry level: salary 40%, benefits 25%, notice 15%
    Senior: notice 25%, clauses 20%, salary 30%
    Legal violations present: legal weight doubles to 20%, others normalized

  6 Context Multipliers:
    🦄 UNICORN CONTRACT: salary>=80pct + notice<=20pct + benefits>=5 + no NC → 1.15x
    ⚠️ Golden Handcuffs: salary>=90pct + NC>=12mo → 0.95x
    🚀 STARTUP ROCKET: has equity + startup industry → 1.08x
    🏢 Standard MNC: salary 40-60pct + notice 30-60d + benefits>=3 → 1.02x
    🚨 TOXIC CONTRACT: 3+ toxic factors → 0.85x
    ⚠️ SERVICE TRAP: notice>=90d + salary<40pct + bond → 0.90x

  Psychological Calibration:
    Score >= 85: compressed by 0.6x (truly great contracts are rare)
    Score 70-85: compressed by 0.85x
    Score 50-70: unchanged
    Score 30-50: softened by 0.9x
    Score < 30: softened by 0.8x (nothing feels like absolute zero)

  Confidence Score: Base 0.50, +0.20 salary percentile, +0.10 notice percentile,
    +0.05 benefits, +0.03 non-compete, +0.05 PF known, +0.03 gratuity known

  Grades: EXCEPTIONAL(90+), EXCELLENT(80+), GOOD(70+), FAIR(60+),
          AVERAGE(50+), BELOW AVERAGE(40+), POOR(30+), CRITICAL(<30)

STAGE 6 — NEGOTIATION PLAYBOOK (negotiation_service.py)
  - Context-aware: Skips salary negotiation for service companies
    (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL, Cognizant, Accenture, Capgemini, etc.)
  - Auto-detects service company from extracted company name
  - Generates for: non-compete, salary, notice period, bond
  - Each point includes: current_term, target_term, rationale,
    success_probability, ready-to-use email script, fallback positions
  - Salary target: min(P75, current * 1.20) — max 20% ask

STAGE 7 — RAG EVIDENCE RETRIEVAL (rag_service.py, evidence_service.py)
  - ChromaDB vector store with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings (384 dimensions)
  - Queries for each extracted clause type + compensation + general
  - Returns top 3 similar precedent clauses per type
  - Similarity = 1.0 - cosine_distance
  - Graceful degradation: 3 consecutive errors → RAG auto-disables
  - Clause types: termination, IP, non_compete, confidentiality, compensation

STAGE 8 — AI NARRATION (llm_service.py)
  - Sends scoring summary to Gemini for 2-sentence professional verdict
  - Rate limiting: 4-second minimum between API calls, 60-second cooldown on 429
  - Retry: 3 attempts with exponential backoff (10s min, 60s max)
  - DETERMINISTIC FALLBACK: If API unavailable, assembles verdict from:
    salary context (percentile-based), risk context, favorable context
  - System never breaks — core product works 100% without any API key

SECTION 6: NOVELTY & WHAT MAKES IT UNIQUE
================================================================================

1. SNIPER LLM ARCHITECTURE (Anti-Hallucination)
   - Industry problem: LLMs hallucinate when given full 30-page documents
   - Our solution: Regex first (deterministic), LLM only on targeted pages
   - Page scoring algorithm selects ONLY the most relevant pages
   - Zero-temperature + JSON-enforced responses
   - NO other tool does this hybrid approach for contract analysis

2. PSYCHOLOGICAL SCORING WITH CONTEXT MULTIPLIERS
   - Not a naive weighted average — detects real-world patterns
   - "Golden Handcuffs", "Toxic Contract", "Service Trap" are novel concepts
   - Psychological calibration compresses extremes (like real human perception)
   - Dynamic weights shift based on role level and industry

3. PROGRESSIVE COHORT BROADENING
   - Statistical technique to ensure meaningful benchmarking even with sparse data
   - Relaxes filters step-by-step until N >= 5 for statistical significance
   - No other contract tool does percentile-based salary benchmarking

4. INDIAN LABOR LAW COMPLIANCE CHECKING
   - Checks for missing PF (Provident Fund) and gratuity (statutory requirements)
   - Flags >48 hour work weeks, >90 day notice periods
   - Context-aware: searches both benefits list AND full contract text

5. CONTEXT-AWARE NEGOTIATION
   - Knows salary negotiation is pointless at TCS/Infosys campus placements
   - Generates different strategies for product vs service vs startup
   - Ready-to-use email scripts with fallback positions

6. FULL DETERMINISTIC FALLBACK
   - System works 100% without any API key or internet connection
   - Regex extraction + rule-based scoring + deterministic narration
   - No AI dependency for the core product

--- TECHNICAL QUESTIONS ---

Q1: Explain your system architecture.
A: Three-tier architecture. React+TypeScript frontend communicates via REST API
   with a FastAPI backend. The backend runs an 8-stage pipeline: Parse → Extract
   → Benchmark → Red Flags → Score → Negotiate → RAG Evidence → Narrate.
   ChromaDB serves as the vector database for RAG. Services use singleton
   pattern via @lru_cache to avoid reinitializing heavy resources per request.

Q2: Why FastAPI over Django/Flask?
A: FastAPI supports native async/await which is critical for our LLM API calls
   and OCR processing. It auto-generates OpenAPI documentation from Pydantic
   schemas. ORJSONResponse provides 3x faster JSON serialization. Pydantic v2
   gives us 30+ typed models ensuring type safety across the entire pipeline.

Q3: How do you handle LLM hallucination?
A: Three defenses: (1) Regex-first extraction — 90% of data is pulled
   deterministically. (2) Sniper targeting — page scoring algorithm selects
   only the 2 most relevant pages, never sends the full document. (3) Strict
   output control — temperature=0.0, topP=0.0, topK=1, forced JSON response
   format. Plus a sanitization pipeline that catches impossible values.

Q4: What is ChromaDB and why did you choose it?
A: ChromaDB is an open-source vector database. We chose it because: (1) It
   runs locally with zero infrastructure. (2) It supports cosine similarity
   search on embeddings. (3) It has metadata filtering for clause types.
   We use all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings (384 dimensions) which provide good
   semantic similarity at low computational cost.

Q5: Explain your caching strategy.
A: Content-addressed caching using SHA-256. The cache key combines the file
   hash + a hash of the user context (role, experience, company type). This
   means the same file analyzed with different contexts produces different
   cached results. Cache is file-based JSON stored in the processed directory.

Q6: How does the scoring engine work mathematically?
A: Five component scores (0-100 each) are computed independently, then combined
   with dynamic weights that shift based on role level. The weighted average
   is multiplied by context multipliers (0.85x to 1.15x based on detected
   patterns). Finally, psychological calibration compresses extremes using
   piecewise linear functions. Confidence is computed from data completeness.

Q7: What embedding model do you use and why?
A: sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2. It produces 384-dimensional
   embeddings, is fast to compute (runs locally on CPU), and provides good
   semantic similarity for English text. It's the standard choice for
   semantic search applications where we need speed over maximum accuracy.

Q8: How do you handle scanned PDF documents?
A: The parser computes text density per page. If average density < 0.1 or
   extracted text < 200 characters, it's flagged as scanned. PyMuPDF converts
   pages to 150 DPI PNGs. Each image is base64-encoded and sent to Gemini
   2.0 Flash Vision API with a strict "extract raw text only" prompt.

Q9: What is Progressive Cohort Broadening?
A: When a user's exact cohort (e.g., "SDE in Tier-2 with 0 YOE at a startup")
   has fewer than 5 data points, we progressively relax filters: first remove
   location, then broaden experience window, then broaden industry. This
   ensures we always have a statistically meaningful sample for comparison.

Q10: How does the Negotiation Playbook know when to skip salary negotiation?
A: It maintains a hardcoded set of 13 known service companies (TCS, Infosys,
    Wipro, etc.) where campus placement salaries are fixed. It also checks the
    company_type context. For service companies, it redirects negotiation focus
    to notice period reduction and bond removal instead.

Q11: Explain your regex extraction approach for salary.
A: Six priority tiers: (1) LPA patterns — highest priority because most common
    in Indian contracts. (2) Explicit CTC mentions. (3) Monthly-to-annual
    conversion. (4) Large INR amounts with currency symbols. (5) Fixed+Variable
    breakdowns. Each tier has context exclusion to avoid matching gratuity caps
    or insurance limits. Values pass through a sanitization pipeline.

Q12: What is the confidence score based on?
A: Data completeness. Base 50% for having a parsed document. +20% if salary
    percentile is available (most important signal). +10% for notice percentile.
    +5% for benefits detected. +3% for non-compete status. +5% for PF status
    known. +3% for gratuity status known. Maximum is capped at 98%.

Q13: How do you ensure the system works without an API key?
A: Every stage has a deterministic fallback. Extraction uses regex. Scoring
    is a mathematical formula. Red flags are rule-based. Benchmarking uses
    local pandas DataFrames. Narration assembles prose from condition checks.
    The DeterminismInfo object in the response reports which mode was used
    for each stage (deterministic vs hybrid vs non-deterministic).

Q14: What is the time complexity of your benchmarking?
A: O(N) where N is the market dataset size (2,795 records). Pandas vectorized
    operations make this very fast. Filter operations are sequential mask
    applications. Percentile computation is O(N log N) for sorting. Total
    benchmark time is typically < 50ms.

Q15: How does clause-aware chunking work?
A: The chunking service splits contract text by section headings (regex for
    "Section X:", numbered clauses, ALL-CAPS headers). Each chunk is classified
    into a clause type (termination, IP, non_compete, confidentiality,
    compensation, general) based on keyword detection in the first 300 chars.
    Chunks exceeding 1500 chars are split at paragraph boundaries.

--- NON-TECHNICAL QUESTIONS ---

Q16: What real-world problem does this solve?
A: Millions of fresh graduates in India sign employment contracts they don't
    understand. They get trapped by Rs.2L+ training bonds, 90-day notice periods,
    and missing statutory benefits. FairDeal democratizes contract intelligence
    that was previously only available through expensive lawyers.

Q17: Who is your target audience?
A: Primary: Fresh graduates (0-2 YOE) entering IT/service sector jobs.
    Secondary: Mid-level professionals switching jobs.
    Tertiary: HR professionals who want to benchmark their own offer letters.

Q18: What are the ethical considerations?
A: (1) Privacy: Contracts are analyzed ephemerally — processed in memory, cached
    locally. Only fragmented snippets reach external APIs. (2) Bias: Scoring is
    deterministic and transparent — every point deduction is explained. (3) No
    legal advice: We clearly position this as an intelligence tool, not legal counsel.

Q19: How is this different from just uploading a contract to ChatGPT?
A: ChatGPT gives you a text summary. FairDeal gives you: (1) Mathematical
    percentiles against 2,795 real salary records. (2) Indian labor law
    compliance checking. (3) Severity-coded risk analysis. (4) Ready-to-use
    negotiation emails. (5) Similar clause precedents from a knowledge base.
    (6) A reproducible, deterministic score — not a different answer each time.

Q20: What are the limitations?
A: (1) Market data is synthetic/curated, not from a live salary API. (2) OCR
    depends on Gemini API availability. (3) Regex patterns are tuned for Indian
    English contracts — may not work well for regional language contracts. (4)
    The 30-contract KB is a proof of concept — production would need thousands.

Q21: What is the future scope?
A: (1) B2B Enterprise Dashboard for HR teams. (2) Real-time salary data
    integration (via Glassdoor/AmbitionBox APIs). (3) Multi-language support.
    (4) Auto-redlining (marking up the PDF with suggested changes). (5) Voice
    AI negotiation coach. (6) Expansion to US/EU labor law standards.

Q22: How did you validate the scoring engine?
A: 30 ground-truth test cases covering service (11), product (11), startup (6),
    and consulting (2) companies. 15 known ordering pairs verify that objectively
    better contracts always score higher (e.g., Google > Wipro). We compute
    pass rate, score distribution, category t-tests, feature correlations,
    and 100% determinism verification across multiple runs.

Q23: What was the most challenging part?
A: Salary extraction from Indian contracts. Indian number formatting (18,00,000
    vs 1,800,000), LPA notation, monthly vs annual ambiguity, and distinguishing
    actual CTC from gratuity caps or insurance limits required 6 tiers of
    progressively relaxed regex with context-aware exclusion windows.

Q24: How do you handle edge cases?
A: (1) Empty file → 400 error. (2) File too large → 413 error (10MB limit).
    (3) Scanned PDF → automatic OCR fallback. (4) Missing salary → scoring
    uses defaults, skips benchmarking. (5) LLM rate limited → 60s cooldown,
    deterministic fallback. (6) ChromaDB errors → auto-disable after 3 failures.
    (7) Bond equals salary → cross-validation clears the false positive.

Q25: Explain the frontend visualization.
A: (1) Score Gauges: animated circular gauges for overall, safety, fairness.
    (2) Bell Curve: normal distribution with your salary position marked.
    (3) Clause Heatmap: color-coded severity map of every contract clause.
    (4) Red Flags Panel: severity-coded cards with recommendations.
    (5) Evidence Panel: RAG-retrieved similar clauses with similarity scores.
    (6) Negotiation Playbook: expandable cards with copy-paste email scripts.
