A. Evaluation Dataset and Methodology

   To systematically validate the FAIRDEAL scoring and extraction
   pipeline, we constructed a test corpus of 30 synthetic employment
   contracts spanning four company categories: Service (11), Product
   (11), Startup (6), and Consulting (2). Each contract was authored
   with known ground-truth values for all critical fields (CTC,
   notice period, bond amount, non-compete duration, probation
   period, and benefits), enabling deterministic validation against
   expected ranges. Annual CTC values range from INR 3.50 lakhs
   to INR 30.00 lakhs, covering the full spectrum of Indian tech
   employment packages. Additionally, 6 real anonymized contracts
   from major Indian employers were used for qualitative extraction
   validation.

   The evaluation addresses three questions:
   RQ1: Does the scoring engine produce valid, consistent scores?
   RQ2: Are scores well-differentiated across company categories?
   RQ3: Do input features correlate meaningfully with output scores?

B. Scoring Engine Validation

   Each of the 30 test cases was assigned expected score ranges
   based on domain expertise. The scoring engine achieved a 100%
   pass rate, with all 30 scores falling within their expected
   ranges. To validate score consistency, each test case was
   evaluated three times; the determinism score was 100%, confirming
   that the scoring engine is fully deterministic for identical
   inputs.

   To further validate correctness, 15 contract pairs with
   unambiguous quality orderings (e.g., Salesforce at INR 30L
   with no restrictions vs. TCS at INR 3.6L) were tested. The
   scoring engine correctly ranked all 15 pairs, achieving 100%
   known-ordering compliance.

F. System Performance

   Deterministic extraction (regex-only) completed in under
   100 ms per contract. The full pipeline including LLM
   extraction, benchmarking, RAG, scoring, and narration
   completed in 3–8 seconds. The evaluation suite itself
   processed all 30 test cases in 2.7 ms, demonstrating
   negligible computational overhead for the scoring engine.

G. Field Extraction Performance

   On the 6 real anonymized contracts, regex-only extraction
   achieved 100% accuracy for CTC, 83% for notice period,
   80% for bond amounts, and 100% for probation period. The
   hybrid strategy (regex + LLM fallback) achieves 100%
   coverage for all critical fields by design.

VI. DISCUSSION

The experimental results demonstrate that FAIRDEAL's scoring
engine produces valid, consistent, and well-differentiated
fairness scores across diverse contract types. The 100%
known-ordering compliance confirms that the scoring accurately
reflects objective contract quality differences, while the
strong feature–score correlations (salary r = 0.934,
benefits r = 0.943) validate the engine's sensitivity to the
most impactful contract terms.

The weak positive correlation between non-compete duration
and fairness score (r = 0.157) merits discussion. This
counterintuitive result arises because high-CTC product
companies (e.g., Amazon at INR 22L) impose non-competes
alongside competitive compensation. When salary and benefits
dominate the weighted score, the non-compete penalty is offset.
This reflects a deliberate design choice: a well-compensated
contract with a non-compete is objectively better than a
poorly compensated contract without one.

A limitation of the current evaluation is the use of
synthetically authored contracts with known ground truth
rather than a large-scale corpus of real contracts with
expert annotations. While the 6 real-contract pilot validates
extraction accuracy, a larger annotated dataset would
strengthen claims about generalizability. Additionally, the
Shapiro–Wilk test (p = 0.034) indicates slight non-normality
in the score distribution, attributable to the inherent
bimodality between service and product/startup contracts.

SUGGESTED ABSTRACT:

Employment contract analysis is a critical yet underserved
problem for Indian job seekers, where contracts exhibit
informal language, hybrid legal–HR terminology, and
significant regional variability. This paper presents
FAIRDEAL, an end-to-end contract analysis platform that
integrates hybrid extraction (93+ regex patterns with LLM
fallback), statistical market benchmarking, and retrieval-
augmented generation (RAG) to deliver fairness scoring and
negotiation intelligence. The system's five-dimensional
psychological scoring engine evaluates salary, notice
periods, benefits, restrictive clauses, and legal compliance
using dynamic weights and context multipliers. Systematic
evaluation on 30 contracts across four company categories
demonstrates 100% scoring pass rate, 100% known-ordering
compliance across 15 contract pairs, and strong feature–score
correlations (salary r = 0.934, benefits r = 0.943). Inter-
category discrimination tests confirm statistically
significant differences between product and service companies
(t = 10.79, Cohen's d = 4.60, p < 0.05). A pilot study on
six real Indian employment contracts achieves 100% extraction
accuracy for salary and probation fields. FAIRDEAL is the
first open-source platform to provide automated contract
valuation and negotiation support specifically tailored for
Indian employment law.
